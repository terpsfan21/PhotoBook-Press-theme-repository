# Parent-focused launch plan

**Audience:** US parents and caregivers of children about 4 to 9, with a strong second audience of families of autistic children and children with sensory or communication differences. Dentists, schools and salons are optional referral partners who share a link.

**How to read the numbers.** Research was done on 19 Nov 2026. Keyword figures are Ahrefs estimates for the US. Advertising benchmarks came from search-result summaries of the cited pages, because the pages themselves could not be opened from this environment; treat them as leads to confirm. Anything marked *estimate* is my own.

## The short version

- **Lead with creators and communities, supported by search.** They are cheap, they build the trust this product depends on, and they produce the reviews and video that everything else needs.
- **Do not lead with paid social.** The benchmark cost to win one e-commerce order on Meta is about $39. The estimated profit on a first softcover is about $18. Paid social only works here once families buy two or more books or you have proven creative.
- **Give away a free printable version of each story** in exchange for an email address. Almost everyone searching these topics wants something free; the personalized printed book is the upgrade.

### 1. Search

- Demand for the exact product is tiny: "dentist social story" about 50 searches a month, "haircut social story" 40, "first dentist visit book" close to zero.
- Broader terms have real volume: "social stories" 4,600, "personalized books for kids" 4,100, "first day of school book" 350, "autism haircut" 200.
- The pages that rank for the niche terms are free resources (nonprofits, clinics, Twinkl, YouTube). No commercial competitor ranks, and the terms are easy to rank for.
- "Personalized books for kids" is dominated by Wonderbly, I See Me and Hooray Heroes; Wonderbly is estimated to spend about $92,000 a month on Google search ads. Do not compete there.
- **Plan:** one helpful guide per title ("What happens at a first dentist visit", "Getting ready for a first haircut"), each with a free printable generic story for an email sign-up. Run a $300 Google test on exact niche terms only (*estimate:* $0.30–$2.00 per click).
- **Wording flag:** "Social Story" is claimed as a trademark by Carol Gray. Use "visit-prep book", "picture story" or "preparation story" in your own copy and check with counsel.

### 2. Social (Meta, TikTok, Pinterest)

- Benchmarks: Meta e-commerce cost per order $38.99, 1.53% conversion (Triple Whale, Aug 2025–Jul 2026). TikTok cost per order $17.07 on the same source. Pinterest about $0.83 per click (secondary sources).
- The editor demonstrates well in a 20-second screen recording: pick a story, change the hair, add glasses, flip the pages.
- **Plan:** post organically from day one (short videos, Pinterest pins for "first haircut" and "first day of school"). Spend $500 testing creator-made video as ads, and stop if a first order costs more than about $18.
- **Policy flag:** Meta restricts ads that imply the viewer has a health condition or disability. Say "for kids who like to know what's coming", never "for your autistic child". Confirm the current policy before running ads.

### 3. Parenting and neurodivergent-family communities

- r/Autism_Parenting (about 109,000 members by a third-party estimate) allows promotion only in its weekly "Self-Promotion Saturdays" thread. r/NewParents bans promotion and surveys. Most Facebook groups are similar: ask the admin first and say you are the maker.
- Real organizations and sites that already publish free preparation stories, and so are natural contacts: And Next Comes L, Autism Little Learners, Pathfinders for Autism, ASERT (paautism.org), CARD at the University of South Florida. Podcasts named in listings (vet each): Tilt Parenting, Moms Talk Autism, The Autism Helper Podcast.
- **Community expectations.** The Autistic Self Advocacy Network's principle is "Nothing about us without us." Many autistic adults prefer "autistic child" to "child with autism" (87% in a 2022 US survey), though not everyone does. Avoid cure or "overcome" language, puzzle-piece imagery, and partnerships with Autism Speaks.
- **Plan:** pay autistic adults and an occupational therapist or speech-language pathologist to review both books before launch, name them on the site, and ask for feedback before asking for sales. Offer review copies to the sites above.

### 4. Creator partnerships

- Typical rates: creators with 1,000–10,000 followers, $10–$200 per post; 10,000–100,000 followers, $100–$500 and up. Vendor figures; they vary widely.
- Sending a free book works best with creators under 50,000 followers. There is no reliable figure for how many will post.
- Affiliate commissions for personalized-book brands run 15–20%. **Shopify Collabs**, which your team already uses on photobookpress.com, handles codes, links and payouts.
- **Rules to set in writing:** creators must disclose the free book or payment clearly (FTC; the brand shares responsibility). Ask them not to state a child's diagnosis or show a child's face unless they choose to. Get written permission to reuse their video as ads.
- **Who to approach:** parent creators, autistic-parent creators, occupational therapists, speech-language pathologists, pediatric dentists and hygienists, and stylists who do sensory-friendly haircuts.

## Sample outreach messages

Replace the bracketed parts. Keep the honesty: say who you are and what you are asking.

**To a parent creator (free book, affiliate code)**

> Hi [name], I'm [your name] from Next Up Books. We make picture books that show a child what a first dentist visit or first haircut might be like, with a character you set up to look like your child, including things like glasses, ear defenders or an AAC device. I liked your post about [specific post].
>
> Could I send you one, free? You design it yourself and see every page before we print it. There's no obligation to post. If you do share it, we'd ask you to mention it was a gift, and we can set you up with a code that gives your followers [10]% off and pays you [15]% of each order.
>
> Happy to answer anything. Thank you for considering it.

**To an occupational therapist, speech-language pathologist or pediatric dentist with an audience**

> Hi [name], I'm [your name] from Next Up Books. We print personalized picture books that walk a child through a first [dentist visit / haircut]: what they might see and hear, and how to ask for a pause. We make no therapy or medical claims; it is a book to read together beforehand.
>
> Before we launch, we want people who do this work to tell us what we got wrong. Would you look through the [dentist] story (about ten minutes)? We pay [$75] for a written review. If you end up liking it, we would be glad to send a copy for your [practice / clients], but the review is what we are asking for.

**To a community moderator or group admin**

> Hi [name], I run a small company that makes personalized picture books to help kids get ready for a first dentist visit or haircut. I don't want to break your rules, so I'm asking first.
>
> Would it be acceptable to [post once in your promotion thread / offer members a free printable version of the story]? I would say plainly that I'm the maker. If the answer is no, that's fine, and thank you for running the group.

**To a dentist, school or salon (optional referral partner)**

> Hi [name], I'm [your name] from Next Up Books. Families use our picture books to show a child what a first [dentist visit / haircut / school day] might be like before the day comes.
>
> If it would help your families, we can give you a link and a small card with a QR code to share in appointment reminders or at the front desk. It costs you nothing, we never ask for patient or student information, and families who use your link get [10]% off. Would you like a sample copy to look at first?

## Proposed launch budget: $3,000 over 90 days

| Item | Amount | Notes |
|---|---:|---|
| Paid reviewers (autistic adults, an OT or SLP, a pediatric dentist, a stylist) | $600 | Before any marketing. Needed for trust and for the copy itself |
| Books for creators and community sites: 25 at about $20 each, printed and shipped | $500 | *Estimate* of your cost, not retail |
| Two paid creator posts | $500 | With written permission to reuse the video |
| Meta or TikTok ad test using creator video | $500 | Stop rule: first-order cost above about $18 |
| Google ad test on niche terms | $300 | Exact-match terms only |
| Community or newsletter sponsorship, or donated copies | $250 | Only where the organizer invites it |
| Sample books and photography | $200 | Real printed books in real hands for the site |
| Contingency | $150 | |
| **Total** | **$3,000** | Excludes your time, the store subscription and art |

A leaner $1,500 version: keep reviewers ($600), 20 creator books ($400), a $300 ad test and $200 for samples.

## Results to track

| Measure | Target for the first 90 days | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Visitors who start the editor | 25% of title-page visitors | Is the idea interesting? |
| Editor starts that reach the last preview page | 50% | Is the book good enough to finish reading? |
| Preview completions that add to cart | 20% | Is it worth the price? |
| Paid orders | 100 | Enough to read the other numbers |
| Cost to win a first order, all channels combined | Under $12 | Leaves profit on a softcover |
| Books per order | 1.3 or more | The series only works if families buy a second title |
| Hardcover share | Record it | Sets the price test |
| Email sign-ups from free printables, and how many later buy | Record it | Tests the "free first, book as upgrade" route |
| Orders by source and by referral code | Record it | Shows which channel and which partners matter |
| Reprints and refunds | Under 3% of orders | Confirms the cost allowance |
| Reviews, and what reviewers and autistic adults say | Read every one | The reason to change the stories |

Targets are starting points, not forecasts. Track counts only; never send names or character choices to analytics.
