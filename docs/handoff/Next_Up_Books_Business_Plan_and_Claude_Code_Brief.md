# Next Up Books
## Market research, pilot business plan, and Claude Code build brief
**Prepared for PhotoBook Press · November 19, 2026 · Working name, pending clearance**

### Decision

Build **Next Up Books**, an illustrated series that helps kids picture what happens during a first experience. The same child can star in every title. A caregiver chooses the child's name, optional age band and pronouns, appearance, and supports. They preview the full story before ordering. Offer an **8.5 × 8.5 inch** book in **hardcover and softcover**. Develop stories with **22–34 interior pages**, with the cover specified separately. Plan a **24-page** first production proof and use **28 or 32 pages** where the story needs more room, subject to confirming binding requirements for each format. Keep a digital edition as an optional pilot test.

Start with U.S. caregivers of autistic children and children with sensory or communication differences, roughly ages 4–9 as an editorial design target. The books should feel welcoming to any child preparing for a new experience. First dentist visit and first haircut prove whether the same character system works across titles. First day of school and first airplane ride broaden the collection. Pediatric dental offices remain the first distribution partner to test. An optional illustrated practice edition can use approved empty-room photos as reference for the entrance or exam room. It is an extension of the general illustrated edition.

Use an original branded collection and product template on the existing PhotoBook Press Shopify store. The child is designed with a visual editor. Child photos, birthdates, and diagnoses are unnecessary.

**Headline:** A little story for a big first.  
**Supporting copy:** Put your child in an illustrated story about what they might see, hear, and do the first time.

### Illustration and personalization

Use warm gouache and paper-cut textures, clear silhouettes, quiet backgrounds, and a shared palette of cream, muted teal, ochre, coral, and leaf green. The sample artwork consists of a four-situation board, a character and accessory board, and an illustrated classroom background and character cutout as a compositing example.

- Put the child's name on the cover and in selected story text. Offer optional pronouns and age bands, such as 4–5, 6–7, and 8–9. Use age bands for an age-appropriate body silhouette where assets exist. Give caregivers a separate easy-to-read text setting. Use first-person narration where it fits. A separate sex field is only needed if an approved story truly uses it. Pronouns and appearance choices cover the current use cases.
- Let families select skin tone, hair style and color, eye color where visible, outfit, and optional glasses, hearing aids, ear defenders, AAC device or mobility aid. Allow a comfort item and a caregiver-approved support if the title supports them. Let caregivers choose features independently. Publish only combinations represented correctly on every relevant page. If wheelchair art is available for a story, use associated seated poses and scene blocking throughout.
- Build stories from **fixed, versioned scene art and editable text**. Each spread has art slots for background, child pose, optional accessory and foreground elements. Keep type editable. Use shared asset IDs, artwork coordinates and overlap rules across Shopify preview and print. For a returning family, offer to carry an existing character into another title within the same session. Any persistent profile needs an adult opt-in and deletion path.
- The boards are visual references. Use the classroom assets to prove layered rendering. Produce standalone high-resolution backgrounds, pose variants and accessories for each approved story, with physical print proofs before release.

### Offer, economics, and distribution

- **Clinic starter offer.** Create a free-to-read office guide and a QR code for appointment confirmations, new-patient emails, and reception desks. Give early practices the option to buy a 25-copy branded pack for their office. Survey willingness to pay at $199 and $249 before committing to a price or a service fee. No patient lists or appointment records are needed for this pilot.
- **Family offer.** Free sample and personalized page preview. Test **$12.99** digital and **$34.99** softcover with digital included. Use **$44.99** as an initial hardcover pricing hypothesis and verify willingness to pay and incremental costs. Show shipping and realistic production timing before purchase. The softcover model assumes paid shipping. Both bindings use the 8.5 × 8.5 inch format and the same approved interior for a title. Confirm separate cover geometry and manufacturing cost for hardcover and softcover.
- **Copy and content.** Use short sentences, one idea per page, illustrated scenes, optional supports, and a predictable format. Explain possible sounds or sensations without promising that an appointment follows a fixed sequence. Give children ways to ask a question, pause, or request a break. Confirm the clinic's actual options. Pay autistic reviewers and a pediatric dental reviewer before using the content with families. Use “visit-prep book” or “visual guide” in public copy. Avoid therapeutic, medical, or anxiety-reduction claims.
- **Distribution.** Start with independent pediatric offices near Upper Marlboro and surrounding Maryland markets. Offer to use five to eight empty-room and equipment photos as reference for an illustrated sample in one short onboarding call. Add search pages for “what to expect at the dentist” only after the partner offer demonstrates use. Defer paid acquisition until there is a measurable contribution margin.

At a 50/50 paid digital and softcover mix, estimated contribution averages **$13.25** per order before acquisition. A **$1,500 pilot cash budget** would take about **114 orders** to recover at that mix, excluding internal labor and ongoing overhead. Budget suggestion is $600 for paid reviewers, $300 for printed samples and shipping, $300 for caregiver interview incentives, and $300 contingency. A sizing example is 20 active clinics × 75 eligible appointment invitations per month × 5% invitation-to-order conversion = 75 orders per month and roughly $994 monthly contribution before partner onboarding or maintenance. These are inputs to measure, not forecasts. Also measure clinic setup time, support minutes, uptake of free guides, and whether clinic packs produce more contribution than caregiver sales.

### 30-day pilot and decision

1. **Week 1.** Interview 12 caregivers and 6 practice staff across several clinics. Ask what families already receive, what caregivers rebuilt themselves, how soon they usually prepare, what images need to match, who approves a guide, and what clinics have paid for comparable materials. Show the generic illustrated dentist book and a location-specific mockup in alternating order. Show the haircut concept as a second title. Use approved empty-room or synthetic photos only as art reference.
2. **Week 2.** Recruit three offices. Make one editable sample per office and show the free guide, digital book, and printed copy side by side. Time onboarding and ask each office where it will distribute the link. Secure photo rights and perform content and print sign-off.
3. **Weeks 3–4.** Run a limited paid pilot after launch authorization. Track aggregate invitations and visits by clinic, preview starts and completions, paid orders by format, returns, support minutes, and print turnaround. Ask caregivers optionally whether they read it before the visit and whether a recognizable location and personalized character mattered. Use aggregated analytics without sending names or selections.
4. **Invest further** if at least three clinics go live, the pilot reaches at least 100 qualified family sessions and 10 paid orders, paid conversion is at least 8% of qualified sessions, print contribution is at least $12 after any shipping subsidy, and at least two clinics want to keep distributing it. These are proposed gates. If traffic is light, extend the test before drawing a conclusion. If families use free versions and rarely buy, test willingness to pay for office packs or clinic-funded guides before building a larger direct-to-consumer catalog.
