# Business plan: direct sales to families

Next Up Books sells personalized picture books straight to parents and caregivers from its own site. Linemark, which owns PhotoBook Press, prints and ships every book. Dentists, schools and salons are optional referral partners.

**How to read the numbers.** Every cost below is an **assumption** until Linemark supplies its own figures. Outside prices were researched on 19 Nov 2026 from search-result summaries of the cited pages (the pages could not be opened directly), so confirm them before relying on them.

## Recommendation

- **Prices:** softcover **$34.99**, hardcover **$44.99**, shipping **$5.99**, free shipping on orders over **$60** (two books).
- **Launch titles:** *My First Dentist Visit* and *My First Haircut*. Add *My First Day of School* in time for July; hold *My First Airplane Ride* until the waitlist shows demand.
- **Expected profit per order** on these assumptions: about **$18** for a softcover and **$21** for a hardcover, before marketing.
- **The business works if** a first order can be won for under about $12 and at least a third of families buy a second title. Both are what the launch is designed to find out.

With six softcovers for every four hardcovers, an average order leaves about **$19.40**. Recovering the proposed $3,000 launch budget takes about **155 orders**. That excludes your time, art, and Linemark's setup work.

**The marketing limit.** If winning a first order costs more than about $18, each softcover loses money. The benchmark cost per order for Meta ads is about $39, which is why the launch plan leads with creators, communities and search instead.

## Which titles first

| Order | Title | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | My First Dentist Visit | Already built in the prototype. Needed all year; visits repeat every six months. Pediatric dental offices are willing referral partners |
| 1 | My First Haircut | Already built. A well-known difficulty for sensory-sensitive children ("autism haircut" about 200 searches a month). Proves the same character works in a second story |
| 2 | My First Day of School | The most-searched topic of the four ("first day of school book" about 350 a month) but seasonal. Art and review must be finished by June to sell in July and August |
| 3 | My First Airplane Ride | Bought less often; sell before summer and winter holidays if the waitlist supports it |
| Later | Swim lesson, eye exam, overnight away | Only after a waitlist test |

Two titles at launch is the minimum that tests the central idea: that a family who made one character will buy that child a second story.

## Testing demand before making more books

1. **Waitlist cards.** Show upcoming titles on the site with "Tell me when it's ready". Commission a title when it has at least 50 sign-ups, or when at least 15% of visitors to its card sign up. Starting thresholds; adjust after the first month.
2. **Ask buyers.** One question on the order confirmation: "What first should we make next?"
3. **Second-title offer.** After a first order, offer the other title with the same character. If fewer than one in five take it within 60 days, fix that before adding titles.
4. **Pre-orders** for a seasonal title (school) once its cover and three sample pages exist.
5. **Search and community signals.** Which free printables are downloaded most; which topics reviewers and parents ask for.

Do not commission full art for a title on instinct. A new title costs art, review and proofing before it earns anything.

## Companion video: a future add-on to test

| Step | What to do | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Interest | Add a checkbox in the editor: "Add a read-along video, +$7.99 (coming soon)". Nothing is charged | What share of buyers want it |
| 2. Price | If at least 15% tick it, make one video for the best-selling title and test $7.99 against $12.99 | What they will pay |
| 3. Decide | Continue only if the extra profit covers the cost of making each video within about 200 book orders | Whether to make more |

Comparable digital extras sell for $7.99 to $19.99 (personalized PDFs and e-books); no competitor price for a companion video was found. Start with one video per title that is the same for everyone; personalized video is a much larger build. Digital products are taxed in about half of US states, so set the add-on up as its own product.

## Risks

| Risk | What to do about it |
|---|---|
| Art consistency. A customizable character needs hundreds of matching layers (see `ASSET_SPEC.md`) | Launch with fewer options; print a proof before release |
| Page count and binding. Outside printers commonly need 24+ pages for hardcover and multiples of 4 for stitched softcover | Confirm Linemark's limits before writing a 22-page story |
| Free alternatives for every topic | Offer the free printable yourself and sell the personalized book as the upgrade |
| Trust. The stories are for families who have been sold to badly before | Pay autistic reviewers and professionals, name them, and make no therapy or medical claims |
| Names. "Next Up Books" is a working name; "Social Story" is a claimed trademark | Clear the brand name; avoid the phrase in your own copy |
| Children's privacy | Adult-facing site, optional first name only, no photos or birthdays; review the FTC COPPA guidance with counsel before launch |
| Personalized books cannot be resold if returned | Reprint for defects or damage; require the "I checked every page" step |

## First 90 days

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Weeks 1–3 | Linemark confirms specifications and costs. Reviewers read both stories. Finished art for the two titles arrives. New store is created |
| Weeks 4–5 | Print proofs in both bindings. Fix what reviewers and proofs show. Test orders in the real store's test mode |
| Week 6 | Quiet launch to reviewers, creators and partner links. No paid ads |
| Weeks 7–12 | Creator posts go out; guides and free printables are published; small ad tests run. Review the numbers in the launch plan every two weeks |
| Day 90 | Decide: continue and add the school title, change price or offer, or stop |

**Go on if** there are at least 100 paid orders, a first order costs under $12 across all channels, each order leaves at least $15 after real costs, and at least one in five families orders a second title.
